# Bake-off #3: free point-in-time data sources (research note)

Researched 2026-10-06. This note only covers data availability. It proposes no strategy and gives no investment advice. Context: [ADR 0002](../adr/0002-bake-off-2-candidates.md) ends with "a Bake-off #3 is only worth running with a genuinely new information source". Window of interest: 2014-01 → 2026-09; the Final Holdout is Oct 2025 → Sep 2026. Bars are UTC (broker = GMT+0), so every US/EU/UK local release time has to be converted with DST.

"Point-in-time safe" means: (a) we can state when each value became public, to the hour, and (b) we can recover the value as first published, not a later revision or a back-cast.

Citations are inline. **not verified** marks a claim I could not confirm at the data owner, and **secondary** marks one found only in a non-owner source.

## 2. US dollar index

**ICE U.S. Dollar Index (DXY).** ICE gives the formula and fixed weights: `USDX = 50.14348112 × EURUSD^-0.576 × USDJPY^0.136 × GBPUSD^-0.119 × USDCAD^0.091 × USDSEK^0.042 × USDCHF^0.036`. It is computed "in real time approximately every 15 seconds" from mid quotes, and it has no regularly scheduled rebalancing ([ICE USDX FAQ, 2015](https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/futures_us/ICE_Dollar_Index_FAQ.pdf)). Since 2021-04-02 it stops at 17:00 ET on Fridays and skips Christmas and New Year's Day ([ICE methodology notice, 2021-03-11](https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/equity_indices/notices/DXY_ICE_FX_Indices_Methodology_Updates_20210311.pdf)). I found no free official history from ICE: real-time and historical values come through market data vendors (FAQ, "Where can I get real time prices"). The same FAQ says any use of the index's "formulation, components, weightings, values … is strictly prohibited without the express written consent of ICE Futures U.S." **Trap (licensing):** a self-computed series with the ICE formula is, on ICE's wording, a use of its formulation. For private research the practical fix is to compute our own geometric dollar basket (e.g. the same six currencies with our own documented weights, or equal weights) and never call it DXY. Whether ICE's clause binds a private, non-distributed computation is **not verified** (a legal question).

- *Data for a proxy:* EURUSD and GBPUSD are already cached. USDJPY, USDCAD, USDSEK and USDCHF would have to be downloaded from Dukascopy too. Whether Dukascopy carries M1 history for all four from 2014 is **not verified** (my test requests to `datafeed.dukascopy.com` failed from this sandbox).
- *PIT:* a basket built from our own bars is causal at bar resolution: no publication lag, no revisions.

**Fed Nominal Broad Dollar Index, `DTWEXBGS`** ([FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DTWEXBGS)). Owner: Federal Reserve Board, H.10 release. Index Jan 2006 = 100, daily.
- *Availability:* "On Mondays at 4:15 p.m. the Federal Reserve Board releases daily bilateral exchange rates and U.S. dollar indexes for the previous business week" ([H.10](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/h10/)). A Tuesday value is therefore public 6 days later. The release of 2026-10-05 covers Sep 28 → Oct 2 ([H.10 current](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/h10/current/default.htm)).
- *Methodology / back-fill:* the 2019 revision rebuilt the indexes, and the Fed gave "a historical series for the new dollar indexes going back to January 2, 2006" ([FEDS Note 2019-01-15](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/revisions-to-the-federal-reserve-dollar-indexes-20190115.htm)). The indexation changed again on 2019-06-24 ([H.10 technical Q&A](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/h10/h10_technical_qa.htm)). Weights "are updated annually" (FEDS Note), and the previous weights ran 2025-03-24 → 2026-01-30 ([H.10 weights](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/h10/weights/)). Whether a weight update restates past index values is **not verified**.
- *ALFRED:* `DTWEXBGS` vintages start **2019-02-04** and are weekly ([ALFRED](https://alfred.stlouisfed.org/series/downloaddata?seid=DTWEXBGS)). The discontinued goods-only `DTWEXB` (Jan 1997 = 100) has weekly vintages 2014-03-18 → 2020-01-06 ([ALFRED](https://alfred.stlouisfed.org/series/downloaddata?seid=DTWEXB)). A PIT series for 2014 → 2026 would mean splicing two different indexes; Jan 2014 to mid-March 2014 has no vintage at all.
- *Verdict:* the weekly lag makes it at best a slow regime variable. For anything faster, a self-built basket from bars is strictly better.

## 3. US real yields: 10y TIPS `DFII10`

- *Owner/source:* FRED lists the source as the Federal Reserve Board, H.15 ([FRED DFII10](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DFII10)). H.15 footnote: "Yields on Treasury inflation protected securities (TIPS) adjusted to constant maturities. Source: U.S. Treasury" ([H.15](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/h15/default.htm)). Treasury derives par real yields from "indicative quotations obtained by the Federal Reserve Bank of New York at approximately 3:30 PM each business day", and has published the series since 2004-01-02 ([Treasury interest rate statistics](https://home.treasury.gov/policy-issues/financing-the-government/interest-rate-statistics)).
- *Availability:* "The release is posted daily Monday through Friday at 4:15pm" ([H.15](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/h15/)). The H.15 dated 2026-10-05 carries data through 2026-10-02 (previous business day) ([H.15 current](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/h15/default.htm)). FRED updated `DFII10` on 2026-10-05 at 3:16 PM CDT (16:16 ET). Safe rule: **value for day D is known from D+1 business day, 16:15 ET**. Treasury's own page posts the same day; a secondary search snippet says "by 6:00 PM", but I could not find that on the Treasury pages I fetched (**not verified**).
- *Revisions:* neither H.15 nor Treasury states a revision policy (**not verified**). Treasury changed the nominal-curve method to monotone convex on 2021-12-06 ([Treasury methodology](https://home.treasury.gov/policy-issues/financing-the-government/interest-rate-statistics/treasury-yield-curve-methodology)); older values were not restated as far as these pages show.
- *ALFRED:* `DFII10` vintages run 2005-10-12 → 2026-10-05 ([ALFRED](https://alfred.stlouisfed.org/series/downloaddata?seid=DFII10)). The page summary suggested ~1,100 vintages, fewer than the business days, i.e. not daily throughout (count **not verified**). Use ALFRED to check for revisions, not as the source of the time stamp.
- *API:* FRED `fred/series/observations` with `realtime_start/realtime_end`, `vintage_dates`, `output_type=4` ("Initial Release Only"). A free API key is required ([FRED API](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/docs/api/fred/series_observations.html), [API keys](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/docs/api/api_key.html)). Treasury XML feed: `…/interest-rates/pages/xml?data=daily_treasury_real_yield_curve&field_tdr_date_value=YYYY`, no key ([Treasury XML feed](https://home.treasury.gov/treasury-daily-interest-rate-xml-feed)).

## 4. Nominal yields and rate differentials

**US `DGS2` / `DGS10`.** Same H.15/Treasury pipeline and timing as `DFII10` (constant-maturity yields "based on the closing market bid yields on actively traded Treasury securities", [H.15](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/h15/)). ALFRED has `DGS10` vintages **daily** from 2005-06-28 ([ALFRED DGS10](https://alfred.stlouisfed.org/series/downloaddata?seid=DGS10)). That makes it the best-documented PIT series here.

**Euro area (ECB).** Curves are "calculated and released every TARGET working day at 12:00 Central European Summer Time (or Central European Time)". History starts 2004-09-06. Inputs are MTS Markets prices; the price used is "the gross price of the bond at the close of the previous business day"; there is an AAA curve (Fitch ratings) and an all-issuer curve ([ECB yield curve methodology](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/financial_markets_and_interest_rates/euro_area_yield_curves/shared/pdf/technical_notes.pdf), [ECB yield curves](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/financial_markets_and_interest_rates/euro_area_yield_curves/html/index.en.html)). Rule: **value for day D is known at 12:00 CE(S)T on the next TARGET day**. Series keys follow `YC.B.U2.EUR.4F.G_N_A.SV_C_YM.SR_<n>Y` (7Y seen at [ECB Data Portal](https://data.ecb.europa.eu/data/datasets/YC/YC.B.U2.EUR.4F.G_N_A.SV_C_YM.SR_7Y); the 2Y/10Y keys follow the pattern, not individually verified). API: SDMX 2.1 REST at `data-api.ecb.europa.eu/service/`, CSV/JSON. A secondary search summary says no key is needed; the API help pages returned HTTP 503 when fetched (**not verified**). Reuse: allowed with the ECB cited as the source, and modifications must be stated ([ECB copyright](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/services/disclaimer/html/index.en.html)). **Trap:** the AAA basket changes with Fitch ratings, so its composition drifts. ECB does not publish vintages; revisions are **not verified**.

**UK (Bank of England).** Nominal, real, inflation and OIS curves; "We aim to publish the latest daily yield curves by noon on the following business day". **"Any updates to archive yield curve data are made available by close of business of the second working day of each month"**, and estimates are revised, e.g. by changes in which instruments are included (a real-curve methodology revision in 2017). The curve files are zip/spreadsheets with no API ([BoE yield curves](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/statistics/yield-curves)). The BoE Database also serves daily series such as `IUDMNPY` (10y nominal par yield) through CSV URLs `…/boeapps/database/_iadb-fromshowcolumns.asp?csv.x=yes&SeriesCodes=…&Datefrom=…&Dateto=now&UsingCodes=Y&CSVF=TN`. Daily rates appear "1-2 working days after the date to which they relate" ([BoE database help](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/boeapps/database/Help.asp)). Licence: Open Government Licence v3.0, with some third-party exchange-rate data excluded ([BoE legal](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/legal)). **Trap:** the downloadable archive is the revised series, not first prints. Use a conservative lag (e.g. D+2) and accept some residual revision risk.

## 5. Economic calendar

**BLS (NFP, CPI).** Schedule times are Eastern; Employment Situation and CPI are at 08:30 ([BLS release calendar](https://www.bls.gov/schedule/news_release/)). Yearly schedules exist back to 1957 at `/schedule/<YEAR>/home.htm` ([BLS archived schedules](https://www.bls.gov/bls/archived_sched.htm)). Better than the *planned* schedule are the archived news-release lists, which show the **actual** release date for every reference month: Employment Situation back to 1994 ([empsit archive](https://www.bls.gov/bls/news-release/empsit.htm)), CPI too ([cpi archive](https://www.bls.gov/bls/news-release/cpi.htm)). Shutdown effects that fall inside the Final Holdout:
- Sep 2025 NFP: planned 2025-10-03, released **2025-11-20**. Oct 2025 NFP: **never published**. Nov 2025 NFP: 2025-12-05 → **2025-12-16**. Jan 2026 NFP: 2026-02-06 → **2026-02-11** (a 2026 lapse) ([BLS revised dates](https://www.bls.gov/bls/2025-lapse-revised-release-dates.htm), [empsit archive](https://www.bls.gov/bls/news-release/empsit.htm)).
- Sep 2025 CPI released **2025-10-24**. Oct 2025 CPI: **not published**. Nov 2025 CPI: 2025-12-10 → **2025-12-18**. Jan 2026 CPI: 2026-02-11 → **2026-02-13** (same sources and [cpi archive](https://www.bls.gov/bls/news-release/cpi.htm)).

**Actual values, first print.** ALFRED keeps vintages: `PAYEMS` from 1955-05-06 (including 2025-11-20 and 2025-12-16) and `CPIAUCSL` from 1972-07-21 (including 2025-10-24 and 2025-12-18) ([ALFRED PAYEMS](https://alfred.stlouisfed.org/series/downloaddata?seid=PAYEMS), [ALFRED CPIAUCSL](https://alfred.stlouisfed.org/series/downloaddata?seid=CPIAUCSL)). `output_type=4` returns initial releases ([FRED API](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/docs/api/fred/series_observations.html)). Caveats: vintage dates are **dates without times** (`fred/release/dates` is date-only, [docs](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/docs/api/fred/release_dates.html)), and `vintagedates` omits release dates on which the series did not change ([docs](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/docs/api/fred/series_vintagedates.html)). Combine the ALFRED date with the BLS 08:30 ET time.

**FOMC.** Historical pages list scheduled and unscheduled actions. For 2020: unscheduled 2020-03-03 and 2020-03-15 (a Sunday) statements, plus notation votes ([FOMC 2020](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomchistorical2020.htm)). Recent statements say "For release at 2:00 p.m. EDT" ([2024-09-18](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20240918a.htm)). The 2020-03-15 one was 5:00 p.m. EDT ([2020-03-15](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20200315a.htm)). The 2014 statements say only "For immediate release" ([2014-01-29](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20140129a.htm)), so the exact 2014–2015 release times are **not verified** from the owner. Minutes come out three weeks after the decision ([FOMC calendars](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm)).

**ECB.** Decisions were published at 13:45 CET until 14:15 CET took over from 2022-07-21 ([ECB press release 2022-06-27](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2022/html/ecb.pr220627~73acedf868.en.html)). The meeting calendar and decisions archive are linked from [Governing Council decisions](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/govcdec/html/index.en.html); archive depth is **not verified**.

**BoE.** The decision is published at 12:00 UK time with the minutes (e.g. the [2026 dates](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/news/2025/september/monetary-policy-committee-dates-for-2026)). There are exceptions: on 2025-05-08 it moved to 12:02 BST ([BoE statement](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/news/2025/may/statement-on-the-timing-of-the-mpr-and-mpc-minutes)). Times before the 2015 format change are **not verified**.

**Consensus / expectations.** None of the official owners publish a per-release consensus. Third-party calendars with consensus figures are commercial or carry terms of use I did not review; I found **no free, clearly licensed per-release consensus history** (unverified as an absence). The free official proxy is the Philadelphia Fed Survey of Professional Forecasters: quarterly, mid-quarter, free downloads, covering CPI inflation, unemployment and Treasury rates ([SPF](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/surveys-and-data/real-time-data-research/survey-of-professional-forecasters)). It is too coarse to give a "surprise" for a single release. **What can be built from official sources alone:** exact event timestamps (BLS archive dates + 08:30 ET; FOMC/ECB/BoE decision times) and the first-print actual value plus the change versus the previous print as first known (ALFRED). That allows event-window features ("is there a tier-1 release in this bar/day", "first-print change in payrolls"). It does not allow actual-minus-consensus.

## 6. CFTC Commitments of Traders

- *Owner:* CFTC. Markets: GOLD (COMEX) code **088691**, EURO FX (CME) **099741**, BRITISH POUND (CME) **096742** ([COMEX futures-only](https://www.cftc.gov/dea/futures/deacmxsf.htm), [CME futures-only](https://www.cftc.gov/dea/futures/deacmesf.htm)).
- *Timing:* "generally published each Friday at 3:30 pm Eastern Time (US), using the data from the immediately preceding Tuesday" ([COT](https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/index.htm)). Holidays shift it by one or two days ([release schedule](https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/ReleaseSchedule/index.htm)).
- *Delays (from [special announcements](https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/HistoricalSpecialAnnouncements/index.htm)):*
  - 2018-12-22 → early 2019: suspended in the shutdown, then caught up with two reports a week from 2019-02-01 ([PR 7864-19](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/7864-19)).
  - Feb–Mar 2023: the ION cyber incident postponed reports.
  - **2025-10-01 → 11-12: interrupted**; publication resumed on Wednesday 2025-11-19 with the report originally due Oct 3, then went chronologically at higher frequency ([PR 9138-25](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9138-25)), and the backlog was cleared by 2025-12-29 ([PR 9147-25](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9147-25)). This sits **inside the Final Holdout**: the positions as of Tuesdays in Sep–Dec 2025 became public weeks late.
  - Whether the early-2026 lapse that delayed BLS also delayed COT is **not verified**.
- *History:* yearly zip files (text/Excel): Legacy from 1986, Disaggregated from Sep 2009, TFF from 2010-07-20 ([historical compressed](https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/HistoricalCompressed/index.htm)). The PRE datasets run Disaggregated/TFF back to 2006-06-13 ([COT](https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/index.htm)), i.e. **earlier than the reports were first published**, so the start of that history is back-filled (irrelevant for ≥ 2014). The files carry the as-of date, **not the publication date**.
- *Revisions:* the explanatory notes do not cover corrections ([notes](https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/ExplanatoryNotes/index.htm)). There was a republication on 2015-07-06 (special announcements). Whether historical files are restated after corrections or trader reclassification is **not verified**. There are no ALFRED-style vintages.
- *API:* PRE at publicreporting.cftc.gov (Socrata). CFTC: "Currently, we are not providing tokens … People who are using the API are generally using it successfully without using a token"; exports in CSV/TSV/XML ([COT](https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/index.htm)). Dataset IDs (e.g. `6dca-aqww` for Legacy futures-only) come from **secondary** sources and are **not verified**: the PRE returned HTTP 403 to both WebFetch and curl from this machine. Rate limits are **not verified**. Licensing: US government data; I found no CFTC restriction on reuse (**not verified**).

## 8. Lookahead traps

1. **Observation date ≠ publication date** (all FRED daily series, COT, ECB, BoE): a value stamped D was public at D+1 16:15 ET (H.15), the next Monday 16:15 ET (H.10), the next TARGET day 12:00 CET (ECB), ~D+1/D+2 (BoE), or Friday 15:30 ET for Tuesday's positions (COT). Joining on the observation date leaks 1–6 days.
2. **Back-cast history:** `DTWEXBGS` before 2019-02 is a 2019 reconstruction; nobody saw those values at the time.
3. **Revised archives:** BoE curves are revised monthly; BLS seasonally adjusted series are revised (first print only via ALFRED). Treasury/ECB revision policy is unknown.
4. **Irregular publication:** 2025 and 2026 shutdowns (BLS, COT), the 2019 shutdown and 2023 ION incident (COT), unscheduled FOMC actions (2020-03-03, a Sunday on 2020-03-15), BoE 12:02 on 2025-05-08. A schedule-based ("every Friday 15:30") timestamp is wrong in exactly the stress periods, including the Final Holdout.
5. **Missing observations:** Oct 2025 NFP and CPI do not exist. Code must not forward-fill them as if they were released.
6. **Date-only vintages:** ALFRED gives dates with no time of day. Add the owner's release time explicitly; never treat a vintage as known at 00:00.
7. **Time zones and DST:** ET, CET/CEST and UK time against UTC bars; the ET–UK DST mismatch weeks shift the gaps.
8. **Composition drift:** the ECB AAA curve basket (ratings), Fed index weights (annual), DXY (fixed since the euro).
9. **Vintage gaps:** `DFII10` ALFRED vintages look sparser than daily; `DTWEXB`/`DTWEXBGS` vintages are weekly and there are none before 2014-03-18.
10. **Contract change:** these series are not on the traded symbol's bars, so `signals(bars)` cannot see them (see below).

## 9. Implications for Bake-off #3

Usable point-in-time over 2014 → 2026, in order of confidence:
1. **Self-built dollar basket** from Dukascopy FX bars: exact PIT, same resolution as our bars, no publication lag. Carries cross-currency dollar information. Needs 4 more pairs downloaded; whether Dukascopy history is complete for them is not verified. Do not name or market it as DXY.
2. **US nominal and real yields** (`DGS2`, `DGS10`, `DFII10`): PIT with a "D+1 16:15 ET" rule; `DGS10` has daily ALFRED vintages to audit revisions. Carries rate level, curve slope and real-rate information (relevant to gold); daily resolution only.
3. **Event timestamps** (BLS actual-release dates + 08:30 ET; FOMC/ECB/BoE decision times) with **first-print actuals** from ALFRED. Carries "scheduled macro risk in this window" and first-print change information. **No consensus**, so no surprise measure.
4. **COT positioning** for 088691/099741/096742: weekly, PIT only after building an actual-publication-date table from CFTC announcements. Carries speculative positioning information.
5. **EUR and GBP rate differentials** (ECB next-TARGET-day noon; BoE with a conservative lag): ECB is usable; BoE has residual revision risk.
6. `DTWEXBGS`: not PIT before 2019-02. Prefer item 1.

Infrastructure each would need (no strategy design implied):
- **Fetchers** per owner (FRED/ALFRED with key, Treasury XML, ECB SDMX, BoE IADB CSV, CFTC PRE or yearly zips, BLS archive pages), resumable like `datafeed/dukascopy.py`, cached locally.
- **An "as-known-at" store:** each row `(series, observation_date, value, available_at_utc, vintage_source)`, with `available_at_utc` computed from the owner's rule plus a hand-maintained exceptions table (shutdowns, unscheduled meetings, holidays). The loader returns, for a bar close `t`, only rows with `available_at_utc ≤ t`, as-of joined, never forward-filling across a missing release.
- **The same holdout guard as `load_bars()`:** auxiliary rows observed after 2025-10-01 hidden unless `include_holdout=True`.
- **A Candidate contract extension:** today `signals(self, bars, **params)` takes one symbol's bars. It would need something like `signals(bars, aux, **params)`, with `aux` already as-of aligned to `bars.index` by the harness, so causality stays the harness's job. `assert_causal` must then also truncate `aux`. Live (`execution/live.py`) needs the same store refreshed on the owners' schedules, so live and backtest still call one function.
- **Trial Count:** every Candidate built on these sources adds to the existing 6.
